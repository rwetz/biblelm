# End-to-end review, 23 October 2026

The study app walked by hand in `pnpm dev` (port 1420), from a cleared
first start through every page, at 1280×800, the 900×600 minimum window, and
375 px. The browser has no Tauri, so the Lab's Python and Axon side and Jev
were not exercised. Notes were made through the browser fallback.

**Caveat on timing:** the browser pane was hidden for most of the run, which
throttles animation frames. Page fades, dialog exits and the scheme crossfade
all ran slowly or stalled there, so anything about animation speed below is
marked unverified.

## What works

Every page renders with real data and no broken images. Checked: the welcome
and name prompt; the Home hero and the verse of the day; search by reference
(`jn 3:16`), word, question (`grace` 170 times), Strong's number (`G26`) and
meaning; the reader with `]`, shift-click passages, the passage panel, notes,
Compare, the interlinear, parallel columns, the Genesis 22 frontispiece and
plate, Look; Topics (index, studies, a study walk from Isaiah 53:5, the map,
structure, words, links, echoes); the Atlas (places, Jerusalem, people,
timeline); the Gallery; Daily (the Office for St James of Jerusalem, Year Two,
correct); Notebooks and the Export dialog; Settings; the palette; all three
schemes.

## Polish

- Compare says "Not in this translation (or numbered differently)" for the
  LXX on a New Testament verse; it could say the LXX is the Old Testament only.
- A note shows its Markdown raw (`**must**`) in the notebook and the panel;
  render it when not editing.
- The selection highlight on verse 1 draws a block behind the drop cap.
- The sidebar vignette can show the same plate that is beside the text
  (Genesis 22).
- The Gallery's subtitle runs over the bright part of its hero painting at
  1280 px.
- The Scripture map's default view is squeezed by a few far outliers (a lone
  "2 Kings"); 1 Enoch's dots have no genre colour or legend entry.
- People: "Judah" reads as the tribe rather than the man (TIPNR merges them);
  two "Lamech"s sit side by side in the timeline without a word to tell them
  apart.

## Ideas

- **Better meaning search without Jev**: fuse OpenBible topic votes into the
  ranking (an "anxiety" query should surface Philippians 4:6 and 1 Peter 5:7,
  which readers vote for, ahead of Mark 13:18).
- **Topics sorted by readers' votes** by default, with A to Z a click away.
- **The Lab's Corpus in the browser**: the study data already has every
  translation, so the corpus preview could work without the desktop app.
- **1 Enoch links**: Jude 14-15 and Enoch in Genesis 5 and the timeline could
  point to 1 Enoch 1:9 when the shelf is on.
- **Look for engravings too**, not only the 25 paintings.
- **Place pages by book**: Jerusalem's 955 verses grouped by book, with its
  first mention marked.
- **An end-to-end test** (Playwright over `pnpm dev`) covering this walk, so
  the next pass is a command rather than an afternoon.

Ideas:

- **Meaning search fuses readers' topic votes** (`votedVerses`, reciprocal
  rank fusion): a topic counts when every word of its name shares a stem
  with the query. "When I feel anxious about tomorrow" now ranks
  Philippians 4:6 third and brings in Matthew 6:25.
- **Topics by votes was not built**: OpenBible's topic scores are not vote
  counts (a few one-passage topics score 100, the median is 3), so ranking
  topics by them put "goobers" first. The index keeps A to Z, names that start
  with a letter first, so it opens on "A Healthy Marriage". Bug 9 is fixed
  this way.
- **The Lab's Corpus works in the browser**: `corpus_list`, `corpus_preview`
  and `corpus_alphabet` read the study texts when there is no desktop
  backend.
- **1 Enoch links** (`src/lib/companions.ts`): Jude 14-15 quotes 1 Enoch 1:9;
  Jude 6 and 2 Peter 2:4 echo 1 Enoch 10:4-6; Genesis 6:1-4 and 5:21-24 are
  retold in 1 Enoch 6 and 1. The verse panel shows them from either side
  under "Beyond the canon", with the passage and a labelled study note.
- **Look for engravings**: a plate beside the text opens in Look (the picture
  or "Look" in its caption); the verse panel credits woodcuts to their
  makers, not "Doré, 1866".
- **Places and people by book**: a long verse list opens with where they are
  first named and a chip per book.
- **The walk as a test** (`src/test/walk.test.tsx`): Vitest over the built
  data rather than Playwright, so it needs no browser download and runs in
  CI after `pnpm data:bible`. It opens every page on its real content and
  searches a reference, failing on any logged error. A browser run
  (Playwright) would still catch layout bugs like 2 and 3, which jsdom cannot.
